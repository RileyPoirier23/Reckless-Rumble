# DRIVEBOSS: game concept

> *Fast & Furious meets Papers, Please, set in a city that gets a real winter.*
>
> You're Leo Covington, a 19-year-old screw-up who inherited his dead father's garage. One night you speed through the wrong part of town and wreck a gang's car. They decide you'll pay it off by turning the garage into their front. Over eight years and eight winters you run the counter, flip cars off MarketThing, learn to drag and drift from parody legends, race the crews that own the city, and find out the crash that killed your dad was no accident.

A 506Studios game, in the same creative family as **CAGE BOSS**: pixel art, R-rated satire, parody characters, paperwork you can't trust, and a story that means something under the jokes.

---

## 0. Pillars

1. **The counter is a weapon.** Every car that rolls into Covington Auto comes with paperwork, and the paperwork lies. The Papers, Please desk from CAGE BOSS is back, now with VINs, registrations, inspection stickers and stolen-car bulletins. Your stamp decides who gets a safety sticker, who gets reported, and whose car quietly gets a new identity in Bay 3.
2. **Cars are characters.** Every car is drawn in more detail than anything in CAGE BOSS: working wheels, suspension that compresses, lights, damage, rust, paint you mixed yourself. Every car has a history, and MarketThing sellers lie about it.
3. **Mechanical truth.** If it would blow a real motor, it blows yours. Burnouts eat tread, a money shift bends valves, and a cold engine revved hard wears out. Winter makes everything harder. Arcade assists exist, but the default is honest.
4. **A city with turf.** The open world is split between crews, CSR Racing 2 style: beat the members, then the boss, and the district changes hands. Parody legends teach you skills, and some of them are the bosses.
5. **Eight years, one kid growing up.** Leo goes from 19 to 27. Seasons change the roads, the cast ages, car prices move, and the city changes around you. The story is funny on the surface and serious underneath, the way Road To Champion was.

## 1. The setting: Port Rumble *(working name)*

A fictional harbour city loosely inspired by the Maritimes. It has a working port, an old airstrip, a mountain road, the highway, back roads, the world's biggest tides and a real winter. It's somewhere Riley knows, so the jokes land and it doesn't look like every other racing game's fake Los Angeles. *(See question 1: we can go full America instead.)*

| District | What it's like to drive | Who owns it (Year 1) |
|---|---|---|
| **Covington Corner** | Narrow old streets, auto row, the garage, the dépanneur, the Tim Burtons drive-thru | Nobody yet. It's yours to hold |
| **Downtown / Main Street** | Grid streets, cruise night every Friday, the most cops | Roman Purse's crew |
| **The Docks** | Container stacks, cranes, gravel, rail crossings | Wreckard Shaw's crew |
| **Northside Industrial** | Warehouses and the dead **Airstrip 7**, home of the drag strip and the long runs | The Familia (Dom Tortellini) |
| **The Heights** | Mansions, gated roads and the tight switchbacks up the hill | Old money and Hatch Motors |
| **Magnetic Hell** | A gravity hill where cars "roll uphill", now a drift spot, plus the mountain pass touge | The Drift Kingpin |
| **The Trans-Canadian** | The highway, the bridge, the long sweepers for highway runs | Whoever's fastest that night |
| **The Back Roads** | Gravel, farms, covered bridges, barns full of forgotten cars | Nobody. Rally country |
| **The Coast Road** | Cliff road and a lighthouse. Where Frank Covington died | Nobody drives it fast. Yet |
| **The Flats** | A beach shortcut that floods at high tide, twice a day | Mother Nature |

**Map size:** a dense, hand-built city rather than a huge empty one. About 4 to 6 minutes to cross at legal speeds, with shortcuts, alleys and parking garages. Every district looks different in each season and at night.

## 2. Leo Covington

- **Age:** 19 at the start, 27 at the end.
- **Who he is:** smart with his hands, terrible with feelings. He smokes weed, drinks too much, drives angry, and is funny in the way people are when they're not okay. He raised his little sister for the last year because nobody else would.
- **His dad, Frank Covington:** a street legend in the 80s who went straight. Twenty years sober, ran Covington Auto, and fixed half the town's cars on credit. He died last December when his car went off the Coast Road in the first snow. **The police report says Frank was drunk.** Leo believes it, and part of why Leo drinks is that he thinks he's turning into his father.
- **The truth (late game):** Frank was stone sober. Someone cut his brake lines, and the report was written to bury it.
- **His sister, Ava Covington:** 14 at the start, 22 at the end. She's the reason Leo can't just run away. Late in the story she learns to drive, and you teach her.
- **Gus Arsenault:** Frank's old mechanic, about 70, grumpy, has worked every Saturday since 1981. He's the Uncle Ray of this game: the mentor who won't say "I love you" but will rebuild your transmission at 2 a.m. He knows more about Frank than he lets on.
- **Mikey Doucette:** Leo's best friend since kindergarten. A loveable stoner, the worst getaway driver in the province, and loyal to a fault.
- **Jules Cormier:** a tow-truck driver who keeps pulling Leo out of ditches. Love interest if you treat her right.

### Leo's vices (systems with consequences, not jokes)

| | Upside | Downside |
|---|---|---|
| **Weed** | Stress goes down; you take bad news at the counter better | Slower reactions for a while, the munchies, and cops smell it at checkpoints |
| **Booze** | Stress drops fast; crews like you more at meets | **Impaired driving:** steering lag, swaying camera, double vision, and checkpoint arrests. A crash while impaired is the worst thing in the game: injuries, a licence suspension, sometimes someone else gets hurt, and the story remembers |
| **Healthy outlets** (unlock over time) | The gym (Uncle Ray cameo), night drives, calling Ava, Gus's fishing trips, and later therapy | Cost time, which is the one thing you never have enough of |

Leo's arc can end sober or not. The game never lectures, but it never pretends either. The prologue crash happens because he's high and drunk, and that is the point.

## 3. The story: eight years, eight chapters

Each chapter is one in-game year with all four seasons. Weeks pass like CAGE BOSS weeks (counter shifts, jobs, nights out), with time skips between big beats ("SIX MONTHS LATER"). Each chapter has a **mentor**, a **crew boss**, a **big set piece**, and a **clue about Frank**.

### Prologue: "Last Call" (Year 1, October)
Leo leaves a party drunk and high in his dad's old beater and is chased by nobody, going 140 through town for no reason. He loses it on a wet corner and lands in the middle of the Familia's Friday meet on Airstrip 7. He totals Mia Tortellini's Supra-alike. Dom Tortellini looks at the wreck, looks at Leo, and says grace.

> "You broke family's car. So now you work for family. Your father's garage? It's a nice garage. Be a shame if it was *legitimate*."

**Tutorial woven in:** your first counter shift (with Gus watching), your first gang work order on a napkin, and your first MarketThing purchase: a $900 car to get around in. "Runs great." It does not run great.

### Chapter 1: "Wrong Turf" (Year 1, age 19)
- **Mentor:** Dom Tortellini teaches the drag race: launch RPM, shift lights, nitrous timing. "I live my life a quarter mile at a time. The rest is paperwork."
- **The garage:** pay back the debt, keep the inspection licence, and get the bays working.
- **Boss:** Johnny Tram (Tier 1 crew, Downtown back streets).
- **Winter arrives:** the first snow, your first slide into a snowbank, winter tires, and a block heater. The anniversary of Frank's death is the first week of December.
- **Frank clue:** Gus mutters that Frank checked his brakes every single Sunday. He'd never have missed it.

### Chapter 2: "Sideways" (Year 2, age 20)
- **Mentor:** Han Lunchbox teaches you to drift in the Heights parkade, a different snack every lesson. "The trick is to never stop eating."
- **Boss:** the Drift Kingpin, in tandem battles down Magnetic Hell in fall leaves (wet, slippery, beautiful).
- **The garage:** first hires and the second bay.
- **Ava:** gets suspended from school, and you have to show up for her.
- **Frank clue:** you find a shoebox of Frank's **cassette tapes** in the attic: voice memos about cars, the town, and Leo as a kid. They become collectibles hidden around the map. A few aren't about cars.

### Chapter 3: "Too Fast, Too Serious" (Year 3, age 21)
- **Bryan O'Corner** walks into the counter with paperwork that's *too* clean. He's undercover. This is a whole chapter of the Papers, Please desk turned on itself: you catch his tells, and you decide whether to burn him, protect him or recruit him.
- **Mentor:** Bryan teaches highway runs: drafting, threading traffic, the long gear.
- **Boss:** Roman Purse (Tier 3, Downtown). He talks for twenty minutes and loses in eleven seconds.
- **Frank clue:** Bryan's file on the Familia mentions Frank Covington, in a report from three years ago.

### Chapter 4: "Five-Oh" (Year 4, age 22)
- **Agent Huge Hobbs** arrives with a federal task force and an eyebrow that can arrest you on its own. Heat peaks, the cops get smarter, and checkpoints go up.
- **Set piece:** the vault job. Two cars, one stolen safe, dragged through Downtown at night. It's the dumbest and best mission in the game.
- **Han "dies"** in a staged crash. #JusticeForHan trends on Bleeter. (He's fine. He's eating chips in a safehouse.)
- **Boss:** a Huge Hobbs pursuit, then a one-on-one drag where he cheats with a giant truck.
- **The garage:** turning legit enough to survive an audit becomes its own minigame.

### Chapter 5: "Forgetti Me Not" (Year 5, age 23)
- **Letty Forgetti** comes back with amnesia. Again. She remembers cars perfectly and people not at all.
- **Ava learns to drive.** You teach her in the parking lot (gentle controls, a patience meter), and she takes her road test at the Ministry office, where you're not allowed to help. It's one of the best scenes in the game.
- **Frank clue:** Frank's crashed car appears on **MarketThing** as a parts car, VIN and all. Someone is selling it. You buy it, put it on the lift, and find the brake line was **cut clean** by a tool, not torn in the crash.

### Chapter 6: "Family Business" (Year 6, age 24)
- **Wreckard Shaw's** crew controls the Docks, and you discover the stolen-car export pipeline running through the port. The cars you stamped DENIED in Year 1 were being shipped overseas.
- **Betrayal:** someone in the Familia has been feeding the pipeline. The family barbecue is very quiet this year.
- **Boss:** Wreckard Shaw (Tier 4), racing through the container yard while cranes move the course.
- **Frank clue:** the pipeline started in the 80s, when Frank and his old co-driver were the two fastest kids in town.

### Chapter 7: "Siphon" (Year 7, age 25)
- **Siphon,** a hacker villain, takes over every car with a modem. Driverless cars rain down from the parkade, and the city is gridlocked. Old cars without computers are the only ones that still work, so your carbureted junk becomes a fleet.
- **Set piece:** the endless runway at Airstrip 7, racing a cargo plane.
- **Han returns.** Justice achieved, snack in hand.
- **Boss:** Siphon (Tier 5), a highway run in a hacked city where the traffic is trying to kill you.
- **Frank clue:** Siphon was hired, and the payments trace back to one company.

### Chapter 8: "The Coast Road" (Year 8, age 26 to 27)
- **The reveal** (proposal; see question 7): **Dale Hatch,** owner of Hatch Motors, the biggest dealer in the province, MarketThing's top seller and the sponsor of the new legal drag strip, the guy who helped you all game. He was Frank's old co-driver. Frank found out Hatch was running the export pipeline and was going to the police, so Hatch cut his brakes in the first snow and had a friend write the report.
- **The final race:** the first snow of the eighth winter, at night, down the Coast Road where Frank died, in Frank's car, which you rebuilt from the MarketThing parts car.
- **Endings,** based on your choices across eight years:
  1. **Clean:** you bring Hatch the evidence and the police. Covington Auto goes fully legit, and Ava runs the counter.
  2. **The street:** Hatch doesn't make the last corner. You become what Dom was.
  3. **Kingpin:** you take Hatch's whole operation and own the city. You're rich, and Gus stops coming in on Saturdays.
  4. **The open road:** you sell the garage and leave with Ava. Ten years later, a postcard.
- **Credits** include the family barbecue, a memoriam card for Frank, the crew, and the friends who are in the game.

**Bryan's ending is handled with care.** The actor who inspired the character died in a car crash, so Bryan's arc ends with him choosing family and driving off down a fork in the road, never with a joke about crashing. (See question 9.)

## 5. The core loop

A day has three parts, and a week ends with bills, like CAGE BOSS.

| Time | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Morning: the counter** | 4 to 8 customers. Read the paperwork, inspect the car, stamp it, and hand the work to a bay. Some customers aren't customers |
| **Afternoon: the city** | Drive to jobs, MarketThing meetups, parts runs, tows, training with your mentor, and story missions |
| **Night: the street** | Meets, races, crew challenges, gang jobs and cruise night. More cops, more money, more risk |
| **Sunday** | Rent, wages, the loan shark, Ava's school fees, the Familia's cut. The week's paper and the Bleeter feed |

**Meters:**
- **Clean cash** (in the bank) and **dirty cash** (in a coffee can).
- **Street rep** and **shop rep** (Yowl reviews).
- **Heat** (police attention).
- **Family trust.**
- **Stress.**
- **Ava** (how she's doing).

## 6. The counter (Papers, Please at a garage)

Covington Auto is a licensed **safety inspection station**. That licence is gold to a gang, because a clean sticker and a clean VIN make a stolen car legal.

### The documents
- **Work order:** what they want, signed.
- **Registration:** plate, VIN, owner, address, expiry.
- **Driver's licence:** photo, name and address. Check them against the person at the counter.
- **Insurance slip:** policy dates and the covered vehicle.
- **The VIN, three times:** dash plate, door-jamb sticker and engine stamp. All three must match the papers.
- **Inspection checklist:** tread depth, brake pads, lights, rust holes, wipers, horn, exhaust.
- **Service history:** odometer readings over the years (spot the rollback).
- **Police bulletins:** stolen plates and VINs this week (BOLO list).
- **Parts invoices:** for insurance claims.
- **Napkin orders:** from the Familia, in code.

### What's wrong (it changes week to week)
Mismatched or ground-off VINs, plates from a different car, expired registrations, a licence photo of someone else, odometer rollbacks, salvage titles "washed" in another province, fresh paint over hit-and-run damage, insurance claims for damage that's obviously older, a forged inspection sticker, bullet holes, a "hidden compartment" nobody mentions, and a customer whose name matches nothing.

### Rules that change
New Ministry bulletins arrive weekly, just like CAGE BOSS:
- Winter tires required Dec 1 to Apr 30.
- New emissions test.
- A stolen-car alert for black Charjers.
- Tint limits.
- Rust-through now fails.

### Your stamps
- **PASS** or **FAIL** (inspection).
- **APPROVED** or **DENIED** (the job).
- **REPORT** (call it in).
- **The wrench:** do it off the books, for the gang.

### Consequences
- **Inspector citations** and fines; enough of them and you lose your licence.
- **Heat and stings:** undercover cops walk in to test whether you'll swap a VIN.
- **Gang trust:** the Familia remembers every DENIED you stamped on them.
- **Reviews:** customers post them on Yowl.
- **Your own mistakes:** a car you passed with bad brakes ends up in the morning paper.

### Working the car (hands-on minigames)
- Read the scan tool's codes.
- **Listen** to an engine and tell rod knock from a lifter tick.
- Measure tread and pad depth.
- Look for rust with the creeper light.
- Color-match paint.
- Bench-build an engine with torque specs.
- Bleed brakes. (Frank's lesson: there's a tape about it.)

## 7. Running the shop

- **Hire mechanics** from applicants. Their résumés and references are documents too, so catch the liar.
  - Skills: engine, electrical, body, paint, tuning, tires.
  - Traits: fast but sloppy, honest (refuses gang work), snitch risk, stoner genius, steals parts, loyal to Frank's memory.
  - Wages, morale and training.
  - Legendary hires: Tej Sparker as your tuner, Gus forever.
- **Bays and equipment:**
  - Lifts, tire machine, balancer, alignment rack, welder.
  - Paint booth (a respray also clears your heat).
  - Dyno, engine stand.
  - Tow truck (tow and repo jobs).
  - A second location; a showroom in the late game.
- **The books:** real invoices vs. "creative" ones. Launder dirty cash through padded repair bills, then survive the audit minigame when the tax man comes.
- **The apartment upstairs:** where Leo lives. Décor changes over 8 years. Ava's room. The garage dog (you can pet him).

## 8. MarketThing

The Facebook Marketplace parody, and the hook: **cars are bought from people, not menus.**

- **Listings:**
  - Photos taken at night in a dark driveway, typo'd descriptions, prices that end in 69.
  - "Runs great needs nothing" (needs everything).
  - "No lowballs I know what I have" (they don't).
  - "Selling for my husband" (he doesn't know).
- **The chat:**
  - "Is this still available?"
  - Negotiate with real offer and counteroffer.
  - Ghosting, scams (fake listings, deposit thieves) and the guy who wants to trade for a jet ski.
- **The meetup:**
  - Drive across the map to the Tim Burtons parking lot.
  - **Inspect the car** like the counter: the VIN against the ownership, oil on the dipstick (milkshake = head gasket), rust, a frame that's been welded, a cold-start smoke check, and a test drive.
  - Sellers lie in their own particular ways.
- **Finds:**
  - Barn finds on the back roads, from old-timers' tips.
  - Estate sales.
  - The guy whose wife said it goes or she goes.
- **Selling:**
  - List your own cars: photos (photo mode), your description, and dealing with the lowballers yourself.
  - Flip cars for profit.
  - Your rep affects buyers.
- **Prices move over 8 years:**
  - Year-1 JDM beaters become Year-8 holy grails.
  - Convertibles are cheap in December.
  - 4x4s spike after the first snow.
  - Gas prices hit V8s.

### Detail (the big upgrade from CAGE BOSS)
- **Wheels:** turn with steering, spin with speed, and smoke and leave rubber marks.
- **Suspension:** squats, dives and body roll.
- **Lights:** headlights, tail, brake and reverse lights, pop-ups that actually pop, and underglow.
- **Exhaust:** flames on overrun, turbo flutter puffs, smoke from a dying engine.
- **Damage:**
  - Dents where you hit.
  - Hanging and missing bumpers, cracked glass.
  - Steam from a holed radiator.
  - Sparks from a dragging exhaust.
- **Wear:** rust that spreads over winters; dirt, salt crust and snow piled on the roof.
- **Paint:** metallic, pearl, matte, two-tone, flake, and a decal/livery editor in pixels.
- **Visual parts:**
  - Bumpers, side skirts, wings (from duck-tail to ridiculous) and hood scoops.
  - Rims (size, style, color, offset).
  - Tint, ride height and camber.

### Performance parts
- **Engine:** intake, exhaust, cams, head work, pistons and rods, displacement.
- **Forced induction:** turbo (size, boost, wastegate, BOV), supercharger, nitrous (shots, purge).
- **Fuel:** injectors, pump.
- **Tune:** ECU tune, timing, AFR, rev limit.
- **Drivetrain:** clutch, flywheel, gear ratios, final drive, LSD, transmission swap.
- **Engine swaps:** the dumb, wonderful kind (a big V8 in a Civil).
- **Chassis:** tires (compound and size), brakes, coilovers, sway bars, alignment, weight reduction, roll cage.

## 10. Driving (realistic by default)

**Camera:** a 3/4 top-down view of the open world that zooms out with speed. **Drag races** switch to a side view, CSR Racing 2 style, with a big tach and shift light. **Drift battles** get a dynamic chase camera.

### Engine
- **The basics:** real torque curves, gear ratios and rev limiters.
- **Money shift:** downshifting into a gear that over-revs the engine floats the valves; bad enough and they bend.
- **Oil:** pressure drops if the oil is low or old. Long hard corners starve it, and **spun bearings** follow.
- **Coolant:** overheats in summer traffic or with a holed radiator, ending in a **blown head gasket**.
- **Knock:** bad fuel with too much boost or timing cracks a piston.
- **Turbo:** lag, boost creep and compressor surge. Too much nitrous on a stock bottom end melts something.
- **Cold starts:** the oil is thick, so revving a cold engine wears it. A rebuilt engine needs breaking in.
- **Winter batteries:** they die in the cold without a block heater.

### Tires
- **Tread depth** in millimetres. **Burnouts eat it** (with gorgeous smoke).
- **Lock the brakes** and you get flat spots.
- **Temperature and pressure** matter. Each compound has a range where it works, and summer tires are useless at -15.
- **Punctures** from construction zones, debris and the nail you didn't see.

### The rest
- **Brakes:** fade with heat, pad wear, warped rotors.
- **Clutch:** wears out from launches. Grinding gears damages the synchros.
- **Suspension:** potholes in spring knock out your alignment, curbs bend wheels, and bottoming out cracks the oil pan.
- **Fuel:** runs out. Wrong fuel is a bad day. E85 and race gas are tuning options.

### Assists, three presets
- **Sim:** everything above is on.
- **Street** (the default for most players): warnings before damage and gentler wear.
- **Arcade:** no mechanical failures.

Every assist can also be toggled separately. Controller rumble follows tire slip and engine knock.

## 11. Racing and things to do

**Race types:**
- **Drag:** 1/4 and 1/8 mile, in side view. Launch, shifts and nitrous; bracket racing; dial-in times; red lights.
- **Drift:**
  - Solo for points; **tandem battles** (lead and chase).
  - Parkade, mountain and docks courses.
- **Sprint and circuit:** through the city with traffic and cops.
- **Touge:** one-on-one down the mountain. Gap ahead wins, gap behind loses.
- **Highway runs:** start at speed, first to pull a set gap ahead wins.
- **Rally:** gravel back roads with a co-driver reading pace notes (Mikey, terribly).
- **Ice racing:** on the frozen lake in January, a real Maritime thing.
- **Pink slips:** winner takes the car, and **you can lose yours.**
- **Cop chases:** wanted levels 1 to 5, PIT maneuvers, spike strips, roadblocks, the helicopter, unmarked cars and the scanner radio. Lose them, or hide in your own paint booth.

**Big set-piece missions (one per chapter):**
- The vault drag.
- The container-yard escape.
- The endless runway.
- The hacked-traffic gridlock.
- The bridge jump (you don't make the bridge jump; Mikey's idea).

**Jobs:**
- Tow-truck calls and repo jobs.
- Parts runs, roadside rescues.
- Pizza delivery (Year 1 money).
- Rideshare ("Hoover").
- Gang deliveries (you don't open the trunk).

**Meets:**
- Friday cruise night downtown.
- Cars & Double-Doubles on Sunday morning, where builds are judged.
- Car shows, dyno days, the county-fair demolition derby.

**The crew ladder (CSR Racing 2 style):** each crew has 5 to 8 members to race in order, then the boss. Beating them flips the district, which changes who hangs out there, what's for sale, and who tries to take it back.

## 12. Seasons and time

| Season | The roads | The town |
|---|---|---|
| **Spring** | Rain, potholes (alignment killers), frost heaves, sand on corners | Mud season; MarketThing explodes with "just took it out of storage" |
| **Summer** | Max grip, overheating in traffic, tar snakes | Race season, cruise nights, tourists, the beach road |
| **Fall** | Wet leaves (ice on wheels), fog, early dark, hurricane leftovers | Hunting season: **moose on the highway at night** |
| **Winter** | Snow, black ice, plow banks, salt. Winter tires or suffer | Block heaters, dead batteries, ice racing, the Coast Road closes |

- **Day and night:** a full cycle with headlights and neon.
- **Weather:** rain, fog, snowstorms (plows clear the main roads first) and freezing rain.
- **Over the 8 years:**
  - The city changes: new highway exits, Hatch Motors grows, a condo tower where the drive-in was.
  - Car trends change and EVs arrive.
  - Prices drift and the radio's music changes.
  - The cast ages: Ava grows up, Gus slows down, Dom goes grey (and denies it).

## 14. Art direction

**Resolution:** 640×360 internal, scaled pixel-perfect. This is more detail than CAGE BOSS's 480×270, but still crisp pixels.

**Recommended look: 3D-rendered pixel art** (see question 2).
- **How it works:**
  - Cars, characters and the city are built as low-poly 3D models.
  - The engine draws them through a pixel-art shader: low resolution, hard toon lighting, 1px outlines, a limited palette.
  - It reads as 2D pixel art. *Dead Cells* used this approach for its characters.
- **Why it's the right call for DriveBoss:**
  - **Cars:** every car can be seen from any angle, with real suspension, steering, damage and every body kit, rim and paint combination. That's impossible to hand-draw for 60+ cars.
  - **Seasons, weather and night** are lighting and material changes, not four hand-painted versions of the whole city.
  - **Characters** animate smoothly, with real walk cycles, driving poses, fights, hugs and lip-sync. That's the big upgrade from CAGE BOSS's rigs. Leo's clothes and hair change across 8 years.
- **Still hand-made where it counts:** UI, portraits, paperwork, MarketThing photos, the newspaper, signs and decals are drawn as pixel art.
- **Cutscenes:** in-engine on built sets with close-up portrait shots, like CAGE BOSS but with animated faces (blinks, eye direction, mouth shapes).

**Palette and mood:**
- Sodium-orange streetlights and neon at night.
- Grey-blue slush in winter, gold leaves in fall, washed-out summer haze.
- The Coast Road always slightly too dark.

## 16. Tech

**Engine: Godot 4** (the same engine CAGE BOSS is being ported to).
- **Strengths:**
  - A real 3D renderer for the pixel-art pipeline.
  - Solid physics.
  - Exports to Windows, Mac, Linux and Android today.
  - iOS with a Mac build machine.
  - Consoles through Godot's console partners, once each platform approves.
  - Free, no royalties.
- **Language:**
  - **GDScript** for gameplay.
  - **C++ (GDExtension)** for the vehicle simulation if it needs the speed (many AI cars, traffic, physics at 120 Hz).
  - The sim is deterministic and testable headless, like CAGE BOSS's balance tools.
- **Data-driven content** (JSON / Godot resources):
  - Cars, parts and torque curves.
  - Documents and discrepancy rules.
  - MarketThing listings, crews, dialogue and the parody bank.
- **Saves:** versioned, with multiple slots, and cloud-ready (Steam Cloud).
- **Performance:** 60 fps on a Steam Deck and mid-range phones.
- **Input:**
  - Controller-first, with rebinding.
  - Steering wheel support later.
  - On phones: touch steering (tilt or virtual wheel) with assists on.

## 18. House rules

- **Parody characters, never actors.** Our names, our designs, nobody's real face or real name.
- **No real car brands or logos.** Parody makes only, drawn to suggest, not copy.
- **No real people in the crime content.** Parodies of real people never appear in crime, doping or abuse content. The movie characters are fictional; we parody them, never the people who played them.
- **Real friends** appear only with their OK.
- **Vices have consequences.** The game never rewards impaired driving.
- **Bryan's ending** is a tribute, never a punchline.
- **Credits** never mention how the game was made with tools; they're about the people.

## 19. Ideas: pick what you want

**Car culture**
